Text version — Eight moves, one resource

Move 1 — Name the person and the moment

Before the rhythm

Who this is for, what they already know, and the situation in which they will need it. Written down in one line before anything else.

What it fixes: content built for an average learner who does not exist.

Move 2 — Write the outcome as something observable, then choose the evidence

Before the rhythm

What the person will be able to do; what would show it. The activity is built to produce that evidence, not the other way round.

What it fixes: outcomes that cannot be assessed — "understand," "be aware of" — and activities that drift from what is finally marked.

Move 3 — Choose the smallest form that works

Before the rhythm

Modality follows the task and the setting — what device, what time, what noise — not preference or precedent. What is left out is decided here and recorded.

What it fixes: courses where a checklist would do; video where a page would do.

Move 4 — Anchor it in the person's own case first

Why & See

Open with their situation and the decision in front of them. Then the model — a worked example before the principle, because a principle without a case has nowhere to attach.

What it fixes: content that is accurate and never used.

Move 5 — Build the practice with its feedback

Try

A task the person can attempt, get wrong, and see the consequence of, with feedback that says what to change rather than whether they were right. Retrieval and spacing are in the schedule, not left to habit.

What it fixes: passive exposure that feels like learning and does not consolidate.

Move 6 — Design the social layer

Share

Where two or more people will meet the resource together, the exchange is structured — what to compare, what to explain to each other — so that expertise circulates rather than sitting with the confident.

What it fixes: discussion as filler; peer learning left to chance.

Move 7 — Close with transfer

Reflect

A prompt that asks the person to name where, in their own work, this applies next — because that naming is what moves knowledge from the resource to the room.

What it fixes: learning that ends when the module does.

Move 8 — Draft accessible, write in plain language, test with the population

Throughout

Access from the first draft, at the reading level of the people named in move 1, and a pass with real learners before release. What they cannot use is redesigned, not explained.

What it fixes: accommodation as a downstream fix; resources that fail and look like a learner who did not try.
